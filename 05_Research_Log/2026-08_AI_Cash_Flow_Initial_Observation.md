# AI Cash Flow Signals — Initial Observation

**Date:** August 2026  
**Status:** Initial observation  
**Companies observed:** Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Broadcom

---

## 1. Why This Project Started

This project did not begin with the question:

> Is AI a bubble?

It began with a much simpler question:

> Where does the cash come from, where does it go, and where might it begin to run short?

The current AI investment cycle is often discussed through themes such as rising capital expenditure, increasing debt issuance, higher bond yields, GPU depreciation, or the possibility that hyperscalers are investing too aggressively.

Those are important issues.

But before reaching those conclusions, there is a simpler constraint.

Economic activity requires cash.

A company can generate cash internally, use accumulated liquidity, issue debt, raise equity, or combine these sources.

The question for this project is therefore not whether AI investment will succeed or fail.

The objective is to observe the movement of cash through the AI economy.

---

## 2. Observation Framework

The initial framework is:

**Operating Cash Flow**

↓

**Capital Expenditure**

↓

**Free Cash Flow / Remaining Cash**

↓

**Liquidity**

↓

**External Funding**

↓

**Funding Conditions**

This creates a possible progression:

### Stage 1 — Cash Generation

Operating cash flow is sufficient relative to investment requirements.

### Stage 2 — Cash Absorption

Capital expenditure grows faster than operating cash flow and absorbs an increasing share of internally generated cash.

### Stage 3 — External Funding

Internal cash generation becomes insufficient for the scale or timing of investment, increasing the role of existing liquidity, debt, equity, leases, or other external funding sources.

### Stage 4 — Funding Stress

External funding itself becomes more expensive, constrained, or difficult to obtain.

These stages are observational categories.

They are not predictions of failure.

A company may remain in Stage 2 or Stage 3 for a long period without experiencing financial stress.

---

## 3. Microsoft — Cash Absorption

Microsoft provides a useful starting point.

Between FY23 Q1 and FY26 Q3:

- Operating Cash Flow increased from approximately **$23.2 billion** to **$46.7 billion**.
- Cash CAPEX increased from approximately **$6.3 billion** to **$30.9 billion**.
- OCF therefore roughly doubled.
- Cash CAPEX increased by almost five times.

The important signal is not that Microsoft is running out of cash.

It is not.

The signal is the relative rate of change.

CAPEX is growing substantially faster than operating cash generation.

CAPEX / OCF increased from approximately **27% in FY23 Q1** to levels above 60% in recent quarters, reaching approximately **84% in FY26 Q2**.

At the same time, Microsoft has not shown a corresponding explosion in debt.

This suggests that Microsoft remains primarily in:

> **Stage 2 — Cash Absorption**

The company continues to generate substantial cash, but a much larger proportion of that cash is being redirected toward infrastructure investment.

---

## 4. Alphabet — Crossing the Internal Cash Boundary

Alphabet shows a more advanced version of the same pattern.

Capital expenditure accelerated sharply through 2025 and 2026.

By 2026 Q2:

- Operating Cash Flow was approximately **$39.1 billion**.
- Cash CAPEX was approximately **$44.9 billion**.
- CAPEX / OCF reached approximately **115%**.
- Quarterly cash remaining after CAPEX became negative.

This is an important threshold.

For that quarter:

> **CAPEX exceeded the cash generated from operations.**

This does not mean Alphabet was experiencing a liquidity crisis.

Alphabet continued to hold very substantial liquidity.

However, the funding structure was changing.

Once CAPEX exceeds current operating cash generation, investment must mathematically be supported by some combination of:

- previously accumulated cash,
- marketable securities,
- debt,
- equity,
- leases,
- or other financing sources.

Alphabet therefore appears to be moving from:

> **Stage 2 — Cash Absorption**

toward:

> **Stage 3 — External Funding**

The distinction is important.

Negative quarterly free cash flow is not equivalent to funding stress.

---

## 5. Amazon — External Funding Becomes More Visible

Amazon shows a similar pattern, but with an even clearer financing dimension.

Cash CAPEX has accelerated rapidly as Amazon expands technology infrastructure, particularly infrastructure supporting AWS.

By 2026:

- CAPEX exceeded Operating Cash Flow in Q1.
- CAPEX again exceeded Operating Cash Flow in Q2.
- Financing cash inflows increased substantially.

This suggests that Amazon has moved beyond simple Cash Absorption.

Its investment requirements are increasingly interacting with its financing structure.

The current observation is therefore:

> **Stage 3 — External Funding**

Again, this is not a statement that Amazon is under financial stress.

Amazon continues to generate very large amounts of operating cash.

The signal is that the scale of investment has become large enough that operating cash flow alone no longer describes the funding mechanism.

---

## 6. Meta — Approaching the Boundary

Meta currently sits between Microsoft and Alphabet/Amazon.

In 2026 Q2:

- Operating Cash Flow was approximately **$31.9 billion**.
- Cash CAPEX was approximately **$30.1 billion**.
- CAPEX / OCF reached approximately **94.5%**.
- Only approximately **$1.7 billion** remained after CAPEX before considering other cash uses.

Meta has therefore not crossed the 100% boundary in this observation period.

But it has moved very close to it.

The current classification is:

> **Late Stage 2 — Deep Cash Absorption**

The important question is not whether one quarter crosses 100%.

The important question is whether the underlying relationship between cash generation and infrastructure investment continues to tighten.

---

## 7. A Common Hyperscaler Pattern

Across Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta, one pattern appears repeatedly:

**Operating Cash Flow remains strong.**

But:

**Capital Expenditure is growing faster.**

This matters because it changes the interpretation of the current AI investment cycle.

The initial signal is not:

> AI demand is collapsing.

It is almost the opposite.

The cash pressure currently visible appears to be associated with the scale of expansion.

In simplified form:

**Strong AI demand**

↓

**Infrastructure expansion**

↓

**CAPEX acceleration**

↓

**Increasing absorption of Operating Cash Flow**

↓

**Lower remaining internal cash**

↓

**Greater potential importance of external funding**

This can be described as:

> **Cash absorption caused by expansion rather than cash deterioration caused by failure.**

That distinction should remain central to this project.

---

## 8. Broadcom — Looking at the Other Side of the Cash Flow

Broadcom changes the perspective.

Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta are primarily observed here as large buyers of AI infrastructure.

Broadcom is observed as a supplier into that investment cycle.

The pattern is almost the reverse.

From FY23 Q1 to FY26 Q2:

- Revenue increased from approximately **$8.9 billion** to **$22.2 billion**.
- Operating Cash Flow increased from approximately **$4.0 billion** to **$10.5 billion**.
- Free Cash Flow increased from approximately **$3.9 billion** to **$10.3 billion**.

At the same time, Broadcom's own Cash CAPEX remains small relative to its Operating Cash Flow.

In FY26 Q2:

- Operating Cash Flow was approximately **$10.5 billion**.
- Cash CAPEX was approximately **$0.23 billion**.
- CAPEX / OCF was only approximately **2.2%**.

Broadcom is therefore converting a substantial portion of revenue into cash without experiencing anything comparable to the hyperscalers' infrastructure capital intensity.

Its reported AI semiconductor revenue has also expanded rapidly.

This creates the first visible connection across the system.

---

## 9. Following the Cash

The initial five-company observation can be simplified as:

**Hyperscalers**

Operating Cash Flow

↓

AI / Data Center CAPEX

↓

**Infrastructure Suppliers**

AI-related Revenue

↓

Operating Cash Flow

↓

Free Cash Flow

From the hyperscaler perspective, cash is being absorbed.

From the supplier perspective, some of that spending appears as revenue and cash generation.

The cash has not simply disappeared.

It has moved.

This is the central observation from the first stage of the project.

---

## 10. The Moving Constraint

This also suggests a broader question.

If hyperscaler operating cash flow can initially finance AI infrastructure, the constraint may be internal cash allocation.

If CAPEX grows beyond internal cash generation, the constraint may move toward liquidity and financing.

If external funding expands substantially, the constraint may move again toward:

- bond-market absorption,
- interest rates,
- credit spreads,
- refinancing conditions,
- or investor willingness to continue supplying capital.

Meanwhile, suppliers receiving that expenditure may experience strong revenue and free cash flow.

The bottleneck therefore does not necessarily remain in one place.

It can move through the system.

---

## 11. What This Does Not Show

The current data does **not** establish that:

- AI investment is a bubble.
- hyperscalers are facing financial distress.
- current CAPEX levels are unsustainable.
- external funding will become unavailable.
- Broadcom's growth will necessarily continue.
- AI infrastructure investment will generate an adequate long-term return.

Those are different questions.

This project currently observes cash movement and funding structure.

It does not yet measure the ultimate economic return on AI investment.

---

## 12. Initial Signal

The first signal from the project is therefore:

> **AI infrastructure investment is absorbing an increasing share of hyperscaler operating cash flow, while parts of that expenditure are simultaneously appearing as rapidly expanding revenue and free cash flow at infrastructure suppliers.**

The current system does not yet look like a simple cash shortage.

It looks like a redistribution of cash accompanied by rapidly increasing capital intensity.

The next question is:

> **Where does the constraint move next?**

That is what this project will continue to observe.
